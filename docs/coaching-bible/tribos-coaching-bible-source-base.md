# Tribos Coaching Bible — Source Base

Evidence review to ground the coach personas' decision policy. Compiled 2026-08-31 from peer-reviewed sources found via web search; each entry lists the papers, the finding with magnitudes where reported, a consensus grade, the strongest counter-evidence, amateur applicability, and draft decision rules expressed in Tribos metrics (RSS, TFI, AFI, FS, EP, EF, PD bests vs 90-day baseline, segment comparisons, subjective readiness).

Consensus grades: **Settled** (multiple RCTs/meta-analyses agree) · **Leaning** (direction clear, magnitude or population uncertain) · **Contested** (credible researchers disagree).

Items marked `[verify]` are from training knowledge, not confirmed in this pass — pull the paper before relying on it.

---

## 0. How to read this for the coach prompt

The coach should never cite a paper by name to a rider. It cites a *rule* with a *confidence level*, and the rule is what these entries produce. Pattern:

> **Rule** → applies when [Tribos signal] → coach says [plain claim] → confidence [Settled/Leaning/Contested] → persona voice varies the delivery, not the claim.

A Contested rule is still usable — the coach just says "the research is split on this, here's the side I lean toward and why." That's more trustworthy than false certainty and is a real differentiator from generic AI feedback.

## 1. Training intensity distribution (TID)

### Key papers
- Seiler KS, Kjerland GØ. *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2006;16:49–56. Descriptive: elite endurance athletes spend ~80% of sessions below LT1. DOI 10.1111/j.1600-0838.2004.00418.x
- Seiler S. *Int J Sports Physiol Perform* 2010;5:276–91. The "80/20" review. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.5.3.276
- Stöggl T, Sperlich B. *Front Physiol* 2014;5:33. 9-week RCT, POL > THR/HIIT/HVT in trained athletes. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2014.00033
- Neal CM et al. *J Appl Physiol* 2013;114:461–71. 6 wk POL > THR in trained cyclists (n=12). DOI 10.1152/japplphysiol.00652.2012
- Treff G et al. *Front Physiol* 2017;8:515. POL not superior to pyramidal in elite rowers. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2017.00515
- Filipas L et al. *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2022;32:498–511. 16 wk POL vs PYR in well-trained runners — order effect (PYR→POL best), neither alone superior. DOI 10.1111/sms.14101
- Burnley M, Bearden SE, Jones AM. "Polarized training is not optimal for endurance athletes." *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2022;54(6):1032–4. DOI 10.1249/mss.0000000000002869
- Foster C, Casado A, Esteve-Lanao J, Haugen T, Seiler S. "Polarized training is optimal…" *MSSE* 2022;54(6):1028–31 (the paired "Contrasting Perspectives" piece).
- Rosenblat MA, Perrotta AS, Vicenzino B. *J Strength Cond Res* 2019;33:3491–500. Early meta-analysis, POL vs THR, only 3 studies. DOI 10.1519/jsc.0000000000002618
- **Silva Oliveira P, Boppre G, Fonseca H. *Sports Med* 2024;54:2071–95.** 17 studies, n=437, GRADE-rated. DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z — the anchor citation.
- Network meta-analysis of individual participant data on TID and VO2max/TT, *Sports Med* Jan 2025 `[verify author — likely Rosenblat et al.]`
- Muñoz I, Seiler S et al. *IJSPP* 2014;9:265–72. POL in recreational runners (the amateur-specific study). DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2012-0350
- Treff G et al. *Front Physiol* 2019;10:707. Polarization Index — a computable definition. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2019.00707

### Findings with magnitudes
- Silva Oliveira 2024 meta-analysis: POL improves VO2peak more than other TIDs, SMD 0.24 [0.01, 0.48], I²=0%, high certainty — but a *small* effect. Superiority held only for interventions <12 weeks (SMD 0.40) and highly trained athletes (SMD 0.46). For time-trial performance: SMD −0.01 (no difference). Time to exhaustion: SMD 0.30 (n.s.). Power at VT2/LT2: SMD 0.04 (n.s.). For trained/developmental athletes: no difference on any outcome.
- Head-to-head POL vs PYR, POL vs THR, POL vs HIIT: no significant differences on any outcome in the 2024 meta.
- Burnley et al.'s critique: TID computed by "session goal" vs "time in zone" gives different answers for the same training; survivorship bias in descriptive elite studies ("building the house around the furniture" — Foster's own concession in the response); POL is ill-defined. Foster et al. conceded the definitional problem.

### Consensus: **Contested on superiority, Settled on the floor**
- Settled: large volume of low-intensity work + some genuinely hard work beats grinding tempo every day (the THR model), for most people, most of the time.
- Contested: whether POL beats PYR. The 2024 meta says no for performance outcomes; the VO2peak edge is small and confined to short blocks in already-fit athletes.
- Strongest counter-evidence to "polarized is best": Silva Oliveira 2024 itself (no TT/threshold advantage), Treff 2017, Filipas 2022 (sequence matters more than model).

### Amateur applicability
Thin and the one direct study (Muñoz 2014, recreational runners) is small. The 2024 sub-analysis found *no POL advantage in trained/developmental athletes*, which is Tribos's population. Time-crunched riders (<6 h/wk) are essentially unstudied. The honest amateur claim is: avoid living in the middle; distribute deliberately; the exact split matters less than people online claim.

### Widely-cited claim that doesn't hold up
"80/20 is scientifically proven optimal for everyone." The 80/20 number is descriptive of elites with 15–25 h/wk. There is no RCT showing it's optimal for a 6-h/wk amateur, and the best meta-analysis finds no performance advantage over pyramidal.

### Draft decision rules (Tribos metrics)
- **TID-1 (Middle-zone drift).** Compute time-in-zone by *session goal* (Burnley) not raw time: if ≥3 of the last 4 weeks have >35% of RSS from tempo/threshold-range rides with no rides clearly easy and none clearly hard → flag "living in the middle." Confidence: Settled that this is suboptimal; Contested what to replace it with.
- **TID-2 (Choose the model by context, not dogma).** Short pre-event block (<12 wk), rider already fit (TFI in upper personal range) → lean POL. Long base or developmental rider → PYR is equally supported and easier to execute. Confidence: Leaning.
- **TID-3 (Don't cite 80/20 as law).** Coach language: "most of your riding easy, a little of it hard" — never a hard percentage unless the rider asks.

### Persona phrasing (same rule, TID-1)
- Scientist: "Your last three weeks were almost all mid-zone. The literature is split on polarized vs pyramidal, but it's clear that this middle band is the worst place to sit. Let's push the easy days easier and put one real hard day back."
- Hammer: "You've been riding medium for three weeks. Medium doesn't build anything. Easy is easy. Hard is hard. Pick one."
- Pragmatist: "Your rides have all been the same effort lately — that's the one pattern the research is clear about. Doesn't need more hours, just needs two of them to be properly easy."
- Encourager: "You've been consistent, which is the hard part. Small tweak: let two of those rides be genuinely relaxed so the one hard one lands."
- Competitor: "Mid-zone every ride is how you show up to a race with no top end. One real hard session, the rest easy — that's what gets you a finish."

## 2. Durability / fatigue resistance

### Key papers
- **Maunder E, Seiler S, Mildenhall MJ, Kilding AE, Plews DJ. *Sports Med* 2021;51:1619–28.** Defines durability as time of onset and magnitude of deterioration in physiological characteristics during prolonged exercise. DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01459-0
- Jones AM. "The fourth dimension: physiological resilience as an independent determinant of endurance exercise performance." *J Physiol* 2023 `[verify journal]`
- van Erp T, Sanders D, Lamberts RP. *MSSE* 2021;53:1903–10. Pro cyclists: maintaining power after accumulated kJ/kg is a key success determinant. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002656
- Mateo-March M et al. "The record power profile of male professional cyclists: fatigue matters." *IJSPP* 2022;17:926–31.
- Muriel X et al. *Eur J Sport Sci* 2022;22:1797–804. Grand Tour durability & repeatability. DOI 10.1080/17461391.2021.1987528
- Spragg J, Leo P, Swart J. *MSSE* 2023;55:133–40. CP fell ~11 W and W′ ~4.7 kJ after fatiguing protocol in pro U23; durability correlated with fresh-state lab markers in pros. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003024
- Valenzuela PL et al. *IJSPP* 2023;18:99–103. Field durability test (20-min TT after ~40 kJ/kg); no correlation with fresh-state markers. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2022-0202
- Gallo G et al. *Biol Sport* 2023;40:485–95. Training dose vs record power outputs, threats to validity.
- Almquist NW, Hansen J, Rønnestad BR. *MSSE* 2023;55:2053–63. Durability develops junior→senior with years of training. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003232
- Smyth B, Maunder E et al. *Sports Med* 2022;52:2283–95. HR:power decoupling in recreational runners — the amateur decoupling paper. `[verify DOI]`
- **Barsumyan A et al. *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil* 2025;17:192.** 20 well-trained amateur cyclists (34.6 y, 8.2 h/wk): 5-min and 20-min power fell 10.8% and 10.1% after 1000 kJ at 80% of 20-min power; *no correlation between durability and VO2max, relative FTP, training volume, years, or age*. DOI 10.1186/s13102-025-01238-8
- Hunter B, Maunder E, Jones AM, Gallo G, Muniz-Pumares D. *Exp Physiol* 2025. Methodological review of durability measurement. DOI 10.1113/EP092120
- Systematic review, *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2025: intensity of prior work, not just volume, drives the durability hit; a trained/developmental group (4.9 W/kg 20-min) showed decrements of 9% (1-min), 5.9% (5-min), 4.1% (20-min) after only 3.5 kJ/kg. DOI 10.1007/s00421-025-05885-0
- Dudley-Rode H et al. *EJAP* 2025;125:1349–59. Carbohydrate ingestion blunts the drop in power at the moderate-to-heavy transition.
- Zanini M et al. *MSSE* 2025. Strength training improves running durability (RCT) `[verify — running, not cycling]`
- IJSPP 2025;20(5):644. Training characteristics vs durability in semi-pro cyclists — *no significant relationship* between higher training load and durability change, large variation across load metrics.

### Findings
- Durability is a real, separable performance dimension. In pros it separates WorldTour from ProTeam and U23 from senior.
- It is *not* predicted by fresh FTP or VO2max in amateurs (Barsumyan 2025) or in a pro field study (Valenzuela 2023); Spragg 2023 found correlations in pros with a harder protocol — protocol-dependent, so treat as Leaning.
- Magnitudes for amateurs: ~10% drop in 5- and 20-min power after ~1000 kJ moderate work. High-intensity prior work costs more than the same kJ done easy.
- What improves it: years of training (Almquist), carbs during the ride (Dudley-Rode), probably strength work (Zanini, running), possibly long rides with efforts placed late — that last one is coaching practice, not yet RCT evidence.

### Consensus: **Settled that it exists and matters; Leaning on how to train it; Contested on how to measure it**

### Amateur applicability
Better than most areas — Barsumyan 2025 and Smyth 2022 are amateur-specific. The gap: no intervention trial in amateurs showing a specific training method improves durability.

### Why this matters for Tribos specifically
Your performance evidence engine already computes EF on steady rides and PD bests vs baseline. Durability is the natural third signal: *the same PD comparison, conditioned on kJ accumulated before the effort*. That's exactly the van Erp method. This is the most under-served finding in consumer apps and maps directly onto gravel/endurance riders, who are a large share of your beta.

### Draft decision rules
- **DUR-1 (Fresh vs fatigued PD).** Tag every PD best with kJ (or kJ/kg) accumulated before it. Report bests in two bins: fresh (<10 kJ/kg prior) and fatigued (≥20 kJ/kg prior). A rider whose fatigued-bin 5-min is >12% below fresh-bin is "low durability"; <6% is "high durability" (thresholds from Barsumyan ~10% mean ±6.5% SD — recalibrate on your own data). Confidence: Leaning.
- **DUR-2 (Decoupling on long rides).** On rides >2 h with steady sections, compute EF in the first vs last third. Decoupling >5% is the conventional practitioner threshold `[no RCT behind the 5% number — practitioner convention]`; Smyth 2022 gives the recreational-runner distribution. Confidence: Leaning on the signal, no consensus on the cutoff.
- **DUR-3 (What to prescribe).** If rider has an endurance/gravel goal and DUR-1 reads low: (a) long ride with the key effort placed after ≥20 kJ/kg, (b) fuel the long ride, (c) don't stack intensity before it. Confidence: Leaning (mechanism-supported, not RCT-proven in amateurs).
- **DUR-4 (Don't over-claim from FTP).** Coach must never infer durability from FTP or a fresh 20-min test. Say so when relevant — it's a credibility point.

### Persona phrasing (DUR-1, low durability, gravel goal)
- Scientist: "Your fresh 5-minute power is solid. After about 1,500 kJ it drops 15%. Durability is independent of threshold power in the research, so this is a separate thing to train — not a fitness problem."
- Pragmatist: "You're strong for the first two hours and then it falls off. That's fixable, and it's the thing that matters for a 6-hour gravel day. One long ride a week with the hard bit at the end, and eat on the bike."
- Competitor: "The race is won in hour five. Your numbers say hour five is where you fade. That's where we train."

## 3. Readiness and autoregulation

### Key papers
- Kiviniemi AM et al. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2007;101:743–51. First HRV-guided RCT (runners). `[verify volume/pages]`
- Vesterinen V et al. *MSSE* 2016;48:1347–54. HRV-guided vs predefined in recreational runners; HRV group had fewer non-responders and tighter outcome spread (−1 to +6% vs −4 to +8% on 3000 m). `[verify pages]`
- Javaloyes A et al. *IJSPP* 2019;14:(1):1–7 and 2020. HRV-guided training in well-trained cyclists — improved 40-km TT and power at VT2; less variance across riders. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2018-0122 `[verify]`
- **Düking P et al. *J Sci Med Sport* 2021;24:1180–92.** Systematic review + meta-analysis: HRV-guided training g = 0.296 [0.031, 0.562] on submaximal parameters (LT1/LT2, VT1/VT2 power), I²=0%; small non-significant effect on performance and VO2peak; *fewer non-responders*. DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2021.04.012 `[verify DOI]`
- Manresa-Rocamora A, Sarabia JM, Javaloyes A, Flatt AA, Moya-Ramón M. *Int J Environ Res Public Health* 2021;18:10299. Methodological meta-analysis: HRV-guided better for maintaining vagal HRV with fewer negative responders; performance advantage "only by a small margin."
- Granero-Gallegos A et al. *Int J Environ Res Public Health* 2020;17:7999. Reports larger VO2max effects but uses within-group ESs — interpret with caution (per Manresa-Rocamora).
- Plews DJ, Laursen PB, Le Meur Y, Hausswirth C, Kilding AE, Buchheit M. *IJSPP* 2014;9:783–90. Minimum 3 valid readings/week averaged approximates a full 7-day mean. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2013-0455 `[verify]`
- Plews DJ et al. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2012;112:3729–41. Elite triathlete case: 7-day rolling Ln rMSSD and its CV; declining CV preceded non-functional overreaching. `[verify]`
- Plews DJ et al. *Sports Med* 2013;43:773–81. "Opening the door to effective monitoring" — Ln rMSSD, rolling averages, SWC = 0.5 × SD.
- **Saw AE, Main LC, Gastin PB. *Br J Sports Med* 2016;50:281–91.** Systematic review of 56 studies: subjective self-report measures reflect acute and chronic training load with superior sensitivity and consistency vs objective measures (HR, HRV, blood markers), and the two generally don't correlate. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2015-094758

### Findings
- HRV-guided training works mainly by *preventing bad days* — fewer non-responders, tighter distribution — rather than by producing bigger average gains. Group-mean advantage is small (g≈0.3 on submax markers, ~0 on performance).
- Single-day HRV is noisy; the 7-day rolling Ln rMSSD with SWC = ±0.5 SD is the standard. Three readings a week suffice.
- Subjective wellness (sleep, fatigue, soreness, stress, mood) beats objective markers for detecting load response (Saw 2016). This is the strongest, least-used finding in the whole review for a consumer product.
- Elite caveat: in very fit athletes, *rising* HRV with flattening CV can signal overreaching, not good recovery (Plews 2012). Probably irrelevant for most Tribos users but the coach shouldn't treat "high HRV = go" as universal.

### Consensus
- HRV-guided training: **Leaning** (positive but modest; protocol matters).
- Subjective > objective for load response: **Settled** for the claim "subjective is at least as good"; the ranking is a systematic-review conclusion, not an RCT.
- Partial sessions being worthwhile: **Leaning** — there is no clean trial on "do 45 min of a 90-min session"; the support is indirect (dose–response of training load; low-volume HIIT literature). `[no direct citation found — say "indirect"]`

### Amateur applicability
Good — Vesterinen and Kiviniemi are recreational runners, Javaloyes are well-trained cyclists. Saw 2016 spans levels.

### Widely-cited claim that doesn't hold up
"Check your HRV each morning and train by the number." Daily values are noise; the RCTs used rolling averages and SWC bands. Any app that changes the day's plan on one reading is not following the evidence.

### Draft decision rules
- **RDY-1 (Ask, don't just measure).** A 2–3 item morning check (sleep quality, fatigue, mood/stress, 1–5 each) is the primary readiness input. Objective HRV is secondary. Confidence: Settled.
- **RDY-2 (Rolling HRV, not daily).** If HRV is available: 7-day rolling Ln rMSSD; normal band = baseline ± 0.5 SD; require ≥3 readings/week to score. One day below band → proceed, maybe trim volume. Two to three consecutive days below band → swap intensity for easy. Confidence: Leaning.
- **RDY-3 (Cut / modify / skip).** Wellness ≤2/5 on two items OR HRV below band ≥2 days, AND AFI high → *modify*: same session, −30–50% duration, keep intensity of the first block. Wellness ≤2/5 on all three, or illness symptoms → *skip*, no negotiation. Wellness fine but "don't feel like it" → *cut to the first 20 min then reassess* (the coach's job is to get them out the door, not to excuse them — Pragmatist voice). Confidence: Leaning; the specific thresholds are Tribos design decisions, not from the literature.
- **RDY-4 (Never override the rider).** If subjective readiness says "bad" and every metric says "good," the coach believes the rider (Saw 2016). Not the reverse.

### Persona phrasing (RDY-3, modify)
- Scientist: "Sleep and fatigue both low today and your recovery trend has been under baseline three days. The evidence says subjective signals like that are more reliable than the numbers. Do the first 20 minutes of the intervals, skip the rest."
- Hammer: "You slept like garbage two nights running. Do the first set. If it's ugly, ride home easy. Don't be a hero on a Tuesday."
- Encourager: "Body's asking for a lighter day and that's information, not weakness. Half the session, first block only, and we'll get the full one Thursday."

## 4. Tapering and recovery

### Key papers
- **Bosquet L, Montpetit J, Arvisais D, Mujika I. *MSSE* 2007;39:1358–65.** Meta-analysis, 27 studies: 2-week taper (ES 0.59 ± 0.33), volume down 41–60% exponentially (ES 0.72 ± 0.36), *maintain intensity* (ES 0.33) and *frequency* (ES 0.35). Cycling/running: optimal volume cut somewhere 21–60%, duration 8–14 d. DOI 10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0
- Wang Z et al. *PLOS One* 2023;18:e0282838. Updated endurance-only meta (14 studies): TT SMD −0.45, TTE SMD 1.28; 41–60% volume reduction (SMD −0.77) with intensity and frequency held; 8–14 d largest effect; pre-taper overload + taper > taper alone. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0282838
- Foster C. *MSSE* 1998;30:1164–8. Monotony (mean/SD of daily load) and strain (weekly load × monotony) predict illness; monotony >2.0 flagged. DOI 10.1097/00005768-199807000-00023
- Murphy C et al. *PLOS One* 2024;19:e0303748. Meta-analysis: overreaching impairs subjective sleep — sleep is a downstream signal of overload, not just a cause.
- Périard JD, Racinais S, Sawka MN. *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2015;25(S1):20–38. Heat acclimation: most adaptation in 7–14 days of daily heat exposure; decays over ~2 weeks without. `[verify — from knowledge]`
- Bonnar D et al. *Sports Med* 2018;48:683–703. Sleep interventions (extension, naps, hygiene) and athletic performance — extension helps; effect sizes vary. `[verify]`
- Recovery between hard sessions: 48–72 h is practitioner consensus; no single anchor RCT found in this pass. `[gap — say "practice consensus"]`

### Findings
- Taper: cut volume ~40–60% over ~2 weeks, keep the intensity, keep the frequency. Expected gain ~2–3% in performance (small-to-moderate ES). Longer than 3 weeks starts to detrain.
- Overload-then-taper beats taper alone (Wang 2023). This is the physiological basis for a "big week then back off" structure.
- Monotony matters independently of total load: the same weekly RSS delivered as identical rides every day is worse than the same RSS with variation.

### Consensus
- Taper structure: **Settled** (two independent meta-analyses agree on direction; magnitudes vary by sport).
- Monotony/strain → illness: **Leaning** (Foster's data are observational; widely replicated in team sport, less in cycling).
- Heat acclimation timeline: **Settled** for the 7–14 day window.
- Sleep: **Leaning** (positive, heterogeneous).

### Amateur applicability
Bosquet's pool is "competitive athletes" of mixed level. Amateurs with low volume have less to cut — a 6 h/wk rider cutting 50% is 3 h, which may under-stimulate. Coach should scale: taper *depth* is proportional to pre-taper load; a low-volume rider tapers by shortening, not by removing days.

### Draft decision rules
- **TPR-1 (Taper prescription).** Event flagged → last 8–14 days: weekly RSS −40 to −60% vs the prior 3-week mean, keep ≥1 short high-intensity session in each of the final two weeks, keep ride frequency unchanged. Low-volume riders (<6 h/wk): cut toward −30% and shorten sessions rather than dropping them. Confidence: Settled (structure), Leaning (low-volume adjustment).
- **TPR-2 (Overload then taper).** If FS is already ≥0 three weeks out, the rider is under-loaded for the taper to have anything to shed. Suggest a build week first. Confidence: Leaning.
- **TPR-3 (Monotony guard).** Weekly monotony = mean(daily RSS)/SD(daily RSS) over 7 days. >2.0 for two consecutive weeks → coach suggests one clearly harder day and one rest day, even if total RSS is fine. Confidence: Leaning.
- **TPR-4 (Heat).** If event forecast is ≥8 °C warmer than rider's 30-day training mean, and ≥10 days remain, suggest 5–7 heat exposures. Confidence: Settled on adaptation window.
- **TPR-5 (Sleep as a signal).** Two consecutive nights of self-reported poor sleep during a build → treat as overload evidence (Murphy 2024), not just as a readiness input. Feed to RDY-3.

### Persona phrasing (TPR-1, two weeks out)
- Competitor: "Two weeks out. Volume drops by half, intensity stays. The research is unusually clear here — riders who keep the sharp stuff and cut the hours arrive fast. Riders who cut everything arrive flat."
- Pragmatist: "Fewer hours, not fewer rides. Keep your Tuesday and Thursday, just shorten them. You'll feel weirdly good by Friday — that's the point."

## 5. Masters-specific adaptation

### Key papers
- Reaburn P, Dascombe B. *Eur Rev Aging Phys Act* 2008;5:31–42. Endurance decline is curvilinear from 35 to ~60–70, exponential after; driven by VO2max ← HRmax and stroke volume; LT velocity falls but *rises relative to VO2max*; economy is maintained. DOI 10.1007/s11556-008-0029-2
- Borges N, Reaburn P, Driller M, Argus C. *J Aging Phys Act* 2016;24:149–57. Recovery kinetics: "the effect of age on performance and recovery may be smaller than originally suggested"; sedentary lifestyle explains much of the observed decrement; evidence on recovery is limited. DOI 10.1123/japa.2015-0021
- Burtscher J et al. *Int J Environ Res Public Health* 2022;19:11050. VO2max decline in masters endurance athletes ranges −5% to −46% per decade; 54% (men) / 39% (women) of the variance explained by *change in training volume*; cessation → up to −20% in 12 weeks; largely recoverable on resumption. DOI 10.3390/ijerph191711050
- Rønnestad BR, Mujika I. *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2014;24:603–12. Heavy strength training improves cycling economy and performance. DOI 10.1111/sms.12104
- Rønnestad BR, Hansen EA, Raastad T. *EJAP* 2010;110:1269–82. 2×/wk for 13 wk builds it; 1×/wk maintains it in-season.
- Rønnestad BR et al. *IJSPP* 2015;11:727–35. Stopping in-season strength work costs performance.
- **Llanos-Lagos C, Ramirez-Campillo R, Sáez de Villareal E. *EJAP* 2025/2026;126:193–222.** Meta-analysis of heavy strength training in endurance cyclists — pull for the pooled ESs on efficiency, pVO2max, TT. DOI 10.1007/s00421-025-05883-2
- Almquist 2023 (durability develops with training age — masters have this in their favor).
- *J Funct Morphol Kinesiol* 2024;9:232. Survey of master cyclists' strength-training practices — most do it off-season and drop it in-season, i.e., exactly backwards from Rønnestad 2015.

### Findings
- The decline is real but much of the *variance* is training, not birthdays. Volume maintenance is the single biggest lever.
- Economy is preserved with age; VO2max is the loss. So high-intensity work stays important for masters (to defend VO2max), not less important.
- Strength training is the best-supported add-on for cyclists at any age and the case is stronger for masters (muscle mass and type II fiber loss). 2×/wk to build, 1×/wk to hold, don't drop it in-season.
- Recovery: the "masters need 48–72 h extra" belief is practitioner lore; Borges 2016 says the evidence is thin and the effect smaller than assumed. Don't over-prescribe rest on age alone.

### Consensus
- Volume maintenance slows decline: **Settled**.
- Strength training benefits: **Settled** for cyclists generally; **Leaning** for masters specifically (extrapolation from mechanism).
- Slower recovery with age: **Contested / under-evidenced**.

### Widely-cited claim that doesn't hold up
"Masters athletes should replace intensity with volume." The opposite: VO2max is what's declining and it responds to intensity. Keep the hard days, manage total load.

### Draft decision rules
- **MST-1 (Don't age-gate recovery).** Rider age ≥40 does not by itself change the recovery rule (RDY-3). Use their measured AFI decay and subjective readiness. If their data show slower AFI decay than population, adjust — but from data, not from age. Confidence: Leaning.
- **MST-2 (Defend the top end).** Masters rider with declining PD 1–5 min bests vs 90-day baseline while EF is stable → prescribe VO2-range intervals, not more base. Confidence: Leaning (mechanism-supported).
- **MST-3 (Strength nudge).** Rider ≥40, no strength sessions logged in 8 weeks, in-season → coach suggests 1×/wk heavy lower-body maintenance. Confidence: Settled (cyclists), Leaning (age-specific magnitude).
- **MST-4 (Break = urgency).** Masters rider with ≥3 weeks off → coach flags that the fitness cost of a break is steep and recovery on return is fast; frame return as high-priority, not as "ease back in for a month."

### Persona phrasing (MST-2)
- Scientist: "What declines with age is aerobic ceiling, not efficiency — yours is holding. The fix isn't more easy miles, it's short hard intervals to defend the top end."
- Hammer: "You're 52, not 82. Your sprint and 5-minute are slipping because you stopped doing them. Do them."

## 6. Coaching behavior science

### Key papers
- Deci EL, Ryan RM. Self-determination theory — basic needs: autonomy, competence, relatedness. Ryan RM, Deci EL. *Contemp Educ Psychol* 2020;61:101860 (the current summary). `[verify]`
- **Mageau GA, Vallerand RJ. ### Findings
- Autonomy support is the strongest, most replicated predictor of sustained motivation. Controlling behaviors (pressure, guilt, conditional approval) actively harm.
- But autonomy support only works with *structure* (clear expectations, rationale) and *involvement* (the coach visibly cares and remembers). A permissive coach with no structure is not autonomy-supportive.
- Goals: specific, moderately hard, self-set or co-set, short-term nested in long-term. "Do your best" is worse than a number.
- Feedback: about the task and the process, not the person. "Your last two intervals faded 8%" > "great effort." Also: feedback must answer *where next*.
- AI-specific: athletes accept AI coaching when it's personalized and goal-oriented; the failure modes are "no personal touch" and, for anxious athletes, feeling exposed. Rationale-giving and remembering history are the cheapest ways to supply "personal touch."

### Consensus
- Autonomy support → motivation: **Settled**.
- Goal-setting effect: **Settled** (small-to-moderate).
- Feedback specificity: **Settled in education, Leaning in sport**.
- AI-coach perception: **Leaning** (pilots only).

### The direct implication for the Strava problem
Athlete Intelligence fails on almost every SDT criterion: no rationale, no choice, no memory (no involvement), self-level feedback ("great ride!"), no "where next." A coach that (a) explains *why*, (b) offers a bounded choice, (c) references what it said last time, and (d) ends with a concrete next step is applying the strongest coaching-science findings available — and none of it requires better physiology.

### Draft coach-behavior rules (persona-independent — these are the floor)
- **CB-1 (Rationale always).** Every prescription carries a one-clause "because." Never a bare instruction.
- **CB-2 (Bounded choice).** Where the evidence permits, offer two options, not one order: "45 minutes easy, or the full 90 if you've got it — your call." Hammer can narrow the frame; it still offers the frame.
- **CB-3 (Task-level feedback only).** Feedback references the ride, the interval, the trend — never the rider's character. "Faded 8% on the last two" not "you gave up." Praise the process ("you held the easy day easy") not the person.
- **CB-4 (Where next).** Every check-in ends with the next concrete action.
- **CB-5 (Continuity = involvement).** Reference the prior conversation or prior ride when relevant ("last week I said we'd revisit…"). This is the single cheapest fix for "no personal touch." Ties to the existing date-agnostic language policy — reference history by event, not by date, unless verified.
- **CB-6 (Goal hygiene).** When a goal is set: make it specific and absolute (a number, an event), moderately hard, and pair it with a 2–4 week sub-goal. Push back on "do my best" and on improbable goals — Kyllo & Landers' improbable-goal ES was zero.
- **CB-7 (Never controlling).** No guilt ("you skipped again"), no conditional approval, no threats. The Hammer is *demanding*, which is structure, not *controlling*, which is need-thwarting. The line: Hammer criticizes the work, never the worth.
- **CB-8 (Confidence honesty).** The coach says which rules are Settled vs Contested when it matters. "The research is split on this" builds trust; false certainty destroys it on the first counter-example the rider reads online.
- **CB-9 (Anxious-rider mode).** If intake or behavior suggests high fear of failure (Dindorf 2025), bias toward Encourager-style delivery on bad news regardless of chosen persona, and lead with what's working before what isn't.

## 7. Gaps and what the literature can't tell you

- **Time-crunched amateurs (<6 h/wk) and TID**: essentially no direct evidence. Coach should say "the research is on people who train more than you; here's the principle that transfers."
- **Durability training interventions in amateurs**: none. Mechanism and pro observational data only.
- **Partial-session value**: no trial. Indirect support only.
- **Masters recovery kinetics**: under-evidenced; don't over-prescribe rest on age.
- **AI-coach efficacy**: pilots measure *perceived suitability*, not adherence or performance. Tribos beta data could be a real contribution here.
- **Women**: most of the above pools are majority male; the TID meta couldn't even run a sex sub-analysis. Flag in coach language where the coach is extrapolating.

## 8. Suggested structure for the bible itself

One entry per rule (TID-1, DUR-1, RDY-3 …), each with:
1. Trigger (Tribos signal, computable)
2. Claim (plain language, one sentence)
3. Confidence (Settled / Leaning / Contested) + one-line reason
4. Citation keys (author-year, resolvable to this document)
5. Five persona lines
6. What the coach must NOT say (e.g., TID-3, DUR-4)

The coach system prompt then references rule IDs and confidence, not papers. The performance evidence engine's verdicts (ahead / consistent / behind / insufficient) become the *trigger* layer; this document is the *policy* layer.
